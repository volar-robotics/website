Company/Purpose

Our purpose · 26 October 2026

Built
to care.

Aerial robots can bring help to an emergency and take tools into hazardous places. We want to build a future in which they help protect people.

A different future for aerial robots.

Think of a drone, and an image of war may come to mind. The asso­ci­a­tion with destruc­tion is under­stand­able. It also gives the people build­ing these machines a respon­si­bil­ity to choose what they are for.

Civil­ian appli­ca­tions offer another direc­tion: deliv­er­ing help, inspect­ing struc­tures and reduc­ing the need for people to enter haz­ardous places. There are already exam­ples of what that can mean in prac­tice.

Emergency care

Help can arrive through the air.

In a Swedish study of sus­pected cardiac arrests, drones deliv­ered an auto­mated exter­nal defib­ril­la­tor (AED) ahead of an ambu­lance in 37 of 55 cases with com­pa­ra­ble arrival times. [1]

Two patients received a shock from a drone-deliv­ered AED before the ambu­lance arrived; one sur­vived to 30 days. The air­craft brought the equip­ment, and people on the ground used it to provide care.

A safer place to work

Care belongs at work, too.

Pro­tect­ing people also means caring about the risks they face at work. Inspec­tors may need ropes, scaf­fold­ing or ele­vated plat­forms to reach a mea­sure­ment point. We want a useful inspec­tion to demand less expo­sure to danger.

HSE's pro­vi­sional figures for Great Britain in 2025/26 record {{ page.hse_falls }} worker deaths from falls from height, out of {{ page.hse_total }} work-related worker fatal­i­ties across all indus­tries. [2]

If a robot can take a mea­sure­ment at an exposed loca­tion, an inspec­tor may be able to do that part of the job from a safer posi­tion. That is a purpose worth working toward.

An ambition for inspection

Bring the tool to the hard-to-reach place.

That ambi­tion extends to con­fined spaces. AUTOASSESS describes sur­vey­ors enter­ing ships' ballast tanks and cargo holds to inspect their struc­tures. Dif­fi­cult access, dark­ness, slip­pery sur­faces and haz­ardous atmos­pheres can make this demand­ing work dan­ger­ous. [3]

The project aims to use aerial robots for inspec­tion and keep sur­vey­ors out of harm's way. It is an example of how the place where a mea­sure­ment is taken could change, while people still need the infor­ma­tion that mea­sure­ment pro­vides.

People at the center

Send the tool.
Keep the expertise.

For us, the purpose of an aerial robot is to support the people doing the work. Their knowl­edge, judg­ment and respon­si­bil­ity remain essen­tial. We want inspec­tors to be able to apply those skills from a safer place.

People choose the task, super­vise the oper­a­tion, inter­pret the read­ings and decide what needs atten­tion. Useful auton­omy should give them better access to evi­dence and the ability to inter­vene.

Our contribution

A useful tool.
A safer position.

At Volar, we are developing tactile interaction for aerial robots. Through SOAR-NDT, we are working toward bringing contact sensing and thickness inspection to hard-to-reach steel. [4]

This work is in development. Practical inspection will depend on validating the full workflow against real requirements. Helping people work safely is the reason to do it.
