Company/Purpose

Purpose · 23 October 2026

Built
to care.

Aerial robots can help protect people and the structures we depend on. That is the work we choose to pursue.

A different future
for aerial robots.

The destructive use of drones in war is one reason to ask what these machines should be built for. Civilian applications give us a clear direction: deliver help, inspect structures, and reduce the need for people to enter hazardous places.

People

Send the tool.
Keep the expertise.

Inspectors sometimes need ropes, scaffolding or elevated platforms to reach a measurement point. An aerial robot could reduce the need for direct access to some of those places.

People still choose the task, supervise the operation and interpret the readings. The goal is a useful tool and a safer position from which to work.

Infrastructure

Care begins
with knowing.

Bridges, ships, dams and wind towers all need inspection. Knowing where steel has lost thickness helps engineers decide what needs attention.

Volar is developing tactile contact and PEC thickness sensing to bring a measurement tool to hard-to-reach steel. Useful inspection will depend on validating the full workflow against real requirements. [4]

Evidence from another application

Help can arrive
through the air.

In a Swedish observational study, drones delivered defibrillators before ambulances in 37 of 55 cases with comparable arrival times. Drones were dispatched for 72 of 211 alerts, and completed 58 deliveries. [2]

This was a different system, not a Volar project. It demonstrates a practical civilian use of aerial robotics; it does not establish a population-wide survival benefit.
