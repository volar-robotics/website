Company/Purpose

Our purpose · 26 October 2026

Built
to care.

Aerial robots can bring help to an emergency and take tools into hazardous places. We want to build a future in which they help protect people.

A different future for aerial robots.

Think of a drone, and an image of war may come to mind. The association with destruction is understandable. It also gives the people building these machines a responsibility to choose what they are for.

Civilian applications offer another direction: delivering help, inspecting structures and reducing the need for people to enter hazardous places. There are already examples of what that can mean in practice.

Emergency care

Help can arrive through the air.

In a Swedish study of suspected cardiac arrests, drones delivered an automated external defibrillator (AED) ahead of an ambulance in 37 of 55 cases with comparable arrival times. [1]

Two patients received a shock from a drone-delivered AED before the ambulance arrived; one survived to 30 days. The aircraft brought the equipment, and people on the ground used it to provide care.

A safer place to work

Care belongs at work, too.

Protecting people also means caring about the risks they face at work. Inspectors may need ropes, scaffolding or elevated platforms to reach a measurement point. We want a useful inspection to demand less exposure to danger.

HSE's provisional figures for Great Britain in 2025/26 record {{ page.hse_falls }} worker deaths from falls from height, out of {{ page.hse_total }} work-related worker fatalities across all industries. [2]

If a robot can take a measurement at an exposed location, an inspector may be able to do that part of the job from a safer position. That is a purpose worth working toward.

An ambition for inspection

Bring the tool to the hard-to-reach place.

That ambition extends to confined spaces. AUTOASSESS describes surveyors entering ships' ballast tanks and cargo holds to inspect their structures. Difficult access, darkness, slippery surfaces and hazardous atmospheres can make this demanding work dangerous. [3]

The project aims to use aerial robots for inspection and keep surveyors out of harm's way. It is an example of how the place where a measurement is taken could change, while people still need the information that measurement provides.

People at the center

Send the tool.
Keep the expertise.

For us, the purpose of an aerial robot is to support the people doing the work. Their knowledge, judgment and responsibility remain essential. We want inspectors to be able to apply those skills from a safer place.

People choose the task, supervise the operation, interpret the readings and decide what needs attention. Useful autonomy should give them better access to evidence and the ability to intervene.

Our contribution

A useful tool.
A safer position.

At Volar, we are developing tactile interaction for aerial robots. Through SOAR-NDT, we are working toward bringing contact sensing and thickness inspection to hard-to-reach steel. [4]

This work is in development. Practical inspection will depend on validating the full workflow against real requirements. Helping people work safely is the reason to do it.
